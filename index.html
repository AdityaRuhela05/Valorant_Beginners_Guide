VALORANT BEGINNER'S GUIDE

Everything you need to know before entering your first match.

New to Valorant? This page explains how to play Valorant in simple language: the basic rules, agent roles, maps, weapons and a few practical tips to help you get started.

Start Learning

Introduction to Valorant

Valorant is a free-to-play tactical shooter made by Riot Games. Two teams of five players fight to win rounds using guns and special abilities.

What is Valorant?

It is a first-person shooter where every player picks an agent, a character with unique abilities. Good aim matters, but so do teamwork, smart positioning and using your abilities at the right time.

5v5 tactical gameplay

Each match is 5 players against 5 players. You only get one life per round, so careful play beats rushing in. The first team to win 13 rounds wins the match.

Attackers and defenders

Attackers try to plant a bomb on a bomb site. Defenders try to stop them. After 12 rounds the teams swap sides, so you learn both roles.

The Spike and rounds

The Spike is the bomb. Attackers win by planting it and defending it until it explodes, or by eliminating all defenders. Defenders win by running out the clock, defusing the Spike, or eliminating all attackers.

Valorant Agents Guide: The 4 Roles

Every agent belongs to one of four roles. Picking a role that fits your play style is the easiest way to start.

Duelist

Duelists are the entry fraggers. They are built to take fights first and get kills so the team can move in. Great if you enjoy aiming and being aggressive.

Controller

Controllers use smokes and other abilities to block enemy vision and control areas of the map. Good for players who like planning and supporting the team.

Initiator

Initiators find enemies and set up the attack. Their abilities reveal or stun opponents, which makes it easier for teammates to get the first kill.

Sentinel

Sentinels guard areas with traps and turrets and can heal teammates. They are strong on defense and are good for patient, careful players.

Beginner idea: try Sage, Sova, Brimstone or Jett first. Their abilities are easy to understand.

Valorant Maps Guide

Every map has the same basic layout, so learn these three areas first:

  • A Site – one of two places where the Spike can be planted.
  • B Site – the second Spike site.
  • Mid – the central area that connects the map and lets you move between sites.

Ascent

An open map with doors in mid that can be closed. Controlling mid makes it easier to attack either site. A good first map to learn.

Bind

Has no mid lane. Instead, two one-way teleporters connect the sites, so listen for the sound of an enemy using one.

Haven

Unusual because it has three sites: A, B and C. Defenders must spread out, and attackers can switch targets quickly.

Split

Narrow lanes and high ground. Mid is very important here, and you can climb ropes to reach better angles.

The map pool changes over time; check the current rotation in-game.

Valorant Weapons Guide

You buy weapons with credits at the start of each round. Weapons are split into categories:

  • Sidearms – pistols. Cheap, and every player starts with a free one.
  • SMGs and Shotguns – good at close range and cheap to buy.
  • Rifles – the most popular all-round weapons.
  • Sniper Rifles – high damage at long range.
  • Machine Guns – lots of bullets, but slow and heavy.

Example weapons

Vandal

Rifle. One headshot kills at any range. Strong and great for beginners learning headshots.

Phantom

Rifle. Fires faster, has less recoil and is quieter. Very forgiving for beginners.

Operator

Sniper. Kills with a single body shot, but it is expensive and slow to fire.

Spectre

SMG. Cheap and fast firing. A great pick for up-close fights and saving rounds.

Buy rounds explained

Eco Round

You buy little or nothing to save credits for the next round. Usually only a pistol.

Force Buy

You spend most of your credits on cheaper weapons and armor even though you can't afford the best, hoping to surprise the other team.

Full Buy

You buy a rifle, full armor and your abilities. This is the normal buy when your team has enough credits.

Weapon details are simplified; costs and stats can change with game updates.

Valorant Tips for Beginners

Small habits make a big difference. Here are six that help the most.

Crosshair placement

Keep your crosshair at head height and aimed where an enemy will probably appear. Less aim adjustment means faster kills.

Movement and shooting

Stop moving before you shoot. Your gun is inaccurate while moving, so stand still or crouch for a moment to fire accurately.

Recoil control

Guns rise as you fire. Use short bursts of 2–3 bullets or pull the mouse down slowly instead of holding the trigger.

Communication

Tell your team what you see, such as "one enemy B Long, low health". Short, calm callouts help the whole team.

Map awareness

Check the minimap often. It shows teammates and enemies they have spotted, so you always know where the action is.

Practice range

Spend a few minutes in the Range before playing. Shoot bots, learn each weapon's spray and test your abilities.
